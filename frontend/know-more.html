Diabetic Retinopathy

Overview

Diabetic retinopathy is a complication of diabetes, caused by high blood sugar levels damaging the back of the eye (retina). It can cause blindness if left undiagnosed and untreated. However, it usually takes several years for diabetic retinopathy to reach a stage where it could threaten your sight.

To minimize the risk of this happening, people with diabetes should:

  • Ensure they control their blood sugar levels, blood pressure, and cholesterol.
  • Attend diabetic eye screening appointments to detect and treat problems early.

How Diabetes Affects the Eyes

The retina is the light-sensitive layer of cells at the back of the eye that converts light into electrical signals. These signals are sent to the brain, which turns them into the images you see.

Over time, a persistently high blood sugar level can damage these blood vessels in three main stages:

  1. Background retinopathy: Tiny bulges develop in the blood vessels, which may bleed slightly but do not usually affect vision.
  2. Pre-proliferative retinopathy: More severe and widespread changes occur, including significant bleeding.
  3. Proliferative retinopathy: Scar tissue and new weak blood vessels develop, leading to vision loss.

Symptoms

Diabetic retinopathy does not usually cause symptoms in the early stages. However, early signs can be detected through eye screening.

Contact your doctor if you experience:

  • Gradually worsening vision
  • Sudden vision loss
  • Floaters (shapes floating in your vision)
  • Blurred or patchy vision
  • Eye pain or redness

Prevention

You can reduce the risk of diabetic retinopathy by:

  • Controlling your blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol.
  • Taking prescribed diabetes medications.
  • Attending all screening appointments.
  • Seeking medical advice for vision changes.
  • Maintaining a healthy diet and exercising regularly.

Treatment

If screening detects significant problems, treatment options include:

  • Laser treatment
  • Injections of medication into the eyes
  • Surgical procedures to remove blood or scar tissue
